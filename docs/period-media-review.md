# Period media: edition and excerpt review

Reviewed October 27, 2026. Scope: the three exact MP4 editions pinned in
[the source locks](../demo/period-source-locks.json), for excerpted looping
public portfolio playback with the original soundtrack.

## Decision

**None of these three editions is cleared for public deployment by this review.**
The archive metadata labels are insufficient to resolve the specific contributions
identified in the films' credits. This is a hold pending evidence, not a finding
that every frame is copyrighted or that permission is impossible to obtain.
The candidate manifest remains unapproved; no reviewer identity is fabricated.

The previous 02:00–02:45 review samples are not final broadcast selections.
In particular, Robot Reality's earlier preview includes the article title page
that its end credits identify as copyrighted and used by permission.
Do not deploy the earlier preview ZIP as a cleared library.

## What was actually checked

- Reacquired originals with the pinned downloader; all three hashes matched the
  previously recorded SHA-256 values.
- Inspected contact-sheet frames across the closing 100 seconds of Robot Reality
  and closing 120 seconds of the other films, at eight-second intervals.
- Inspected twelve frames per candidate from 03:00 through 06:40, every twenty
  seconds, to identify four-minute review windows (03:00–07:00).
- Decoded each four-minute window's audio for level checks. These are technical
  non-silence checks, not a complete listening assessment or music identification.
- Consulted the official CDC reuse page and historical NWS/NTIS catalogs below.

This was not a continuous full-film watch/listen. The windows below are editorial
candidates, not final sentence-accurate cuts. A continuous audiovisual review and
boundary refinement are still needed if the rights questions are resolved.

## Robot Reality — hold for mixed-source footage

Exact file: `gov.ntis.AVA19272/ava19272.vnb1_512kb.mp4`.
Archive year: 1990. Source: https://archive.org/details/gov.ntis.AVA19272.

Closing frames around 14:00–15:15 show:

- Motion-picture material attributed to Metropolis and another scene attributed
  to the Smithsonian's Museum of American History. Those historical inserts
  also conflict with the underlying-footage-era brief.
- A Marvel cover identified as copyright 1974, all rights reserved, used by
  permission, and an article title page identified as copyright 1985, also used
  by permission. A grant to the film producer is not evidence of a grant to us.
- A statement that portions were produced by Cincinnati Milacron and IBM.
  The credits do not map these portions to exact timestamps.

**Proposed window:** 03:00–07:00, industrial equipment, safety graphics and early
computer animation. Sampled frames fit the desired period look. Removing the
opening artwork does not resolve the ownership of the remaining industrial
footage. Do not claim this window is federal-employee-only material.

**Needed to release:** CDC/NIOSH or the film's rights custodian must identify
whether this exact window and its original sound may be publicly hosted,
transcoded, excerpted and looped by a portfolio site, including the IBM and
Cincinnati Milacron contributions. Obtain a documented public-domain basis or
permission covering the intended use, or a timestamp map of independently
reusable footage. Confirm the correct agency credit: the archive lists NIH,
but the on-screen program is associated with NIOSH; do not perpetuate that
metadata ambiguity as a verified public credit.

CDC's current [agency-materials policy](https://www.cdc.gov/other/agencymaterials.html)
notes exceptions for contractor, grantee and licensed content. It also describes
attribution, non-endorsement and content-integrity conditions. This general page
does not resolve the exact film's contributed footage or establish that a
particular excerpt satisfies those conditions.

## Awesome Power — hold for production and excerpt-use confirmation

Exact file: `gov.ntis.ava17096vnb1/gov.ntis.ava17096vnb1_512kb.mp4`.
Archive year: 1988. Source: https://archive.org/details/gov.ntis.ava17096vnb1.

Closing frames around 16:00–17:17 identify NOAA/Department of Commerce as presenter,
Frank A. Maniglia and Robert R. Denny as producers, and Thirtyfive-Sixteen Inc.
as the production company. The credits thank WMTW, KENS, KMOL and KSAT and show
a 1988 Department of Commerce/NOAA copyright notice. The presence of the notice
does not by itself settle copyright validity, but it conflicts with treating the
file as automatically unrestricted. The credits do not identify which shots
came from the stations or the scope of their licenses.

The official [NWS Spring 2000 Aware catalog](https://www.weather.gov/media/publications/Aware/00spring-aware.PDF)
lists this title as a 1988 color, 17-minute VHS, product AVA17096.VNB1. It directs
those using portions of NWS videotapes to NOAA's video studio. This is historical
contact guidance, not a newly verified contact phone number or a license grant.

**Proposed window:** 03:00–07:00, title transition, outdoor presenter, flood damage
and interviews. Visually promising period weather programming; source ownership
of the individual flood shots remains unmapped.

**Needed to release:** NOAA's audiovisual/rights custodian should confirm reuse
of this edition and selected window, including producer/station footage and
soundtrack, for public portfolio streaming, edits/transcoding and continuous
looping. Clarify whether its copyright notice applies to third-party material,
and any required credits or other restrictions. A current starting point is the
[NOAA Library](https://library.noaa.gov/), not an unverified 2000 telephone number.

## Of Ice and Fire — hold for soundtrack and production-rights confirmation

Exact file: `gov.ntis.a19389vnb1/a19389vnb1_512kb.mp4`.
Source: https://archive.org/details/gov.ntis.a19389vnb1.
The official [1998 NTIS educational catalog, PDF page 30](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED461387.pdf)
identifies the Forest Service Pacific Southwest Region film as a 1992,
20-minute VHS. This supports the edition year, not the rights to every element.

Closing frames around 19:00–20:52 credit Bob Tribble (writer/director), Larry Ford
(cinematographer), Rod Harbour and Gwen Bailey-Harbour (original score), and
Alan Blaver and Richard Blaver (Native American music). Other production and
historical-still contributors are listed. These credits alone do not establish
which contributors were federal employees or what reuse rights were acquired.
There is no evidence here that the music is definitely prohibited; there is
also no verified license scope for our reuse of the complete soundtrack.

**Proposed window:** 03:00–07:00, lake ecology, brine shrimp, flies and birds.
Sampled images are color; the low-resolution transfer is dark and somewhat muted.
This avoids the later history/interview portion in the inspected samples, but
sampling cannot establish absence of all historical material or music cues.

**Needed to release:** Forest Service Pacific Southwest audiovisual staff or the
Mono Basin/Inyo National Forest film custodian should confirm the complete-film
and excerpt reuse basis, including the credited score and additional music.
Request the exact rights statement or contract/license terms covering public
hosting and retained soundtrack; do not infer permission from an official upload.

clearance. Final deployment and a three-channel release remain blocked; the
